# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Judges of the 8th Annual Datathon for Social Good (Advanced Track), reading alone on a laptop, with no speaker. They are data-literate, short on time, and comparing many entries. Secondary readers: housing acquisition programs, tenant organizations, and researchers who might use the model.

## Product Purpose

A single scrolling page that makes one argument: the Bay Area boom moves people out through rents at tenant turnover, not through layoffs, and a unit-level model can flag which affordable rentals will be priced out of reach next. Success is a judge finishing the page in a few minutes, able to restate the thesis, the model's result against the legal rule, and who the model still misses. Depth lives in the linked atlas, workstream sites and FINDINGS.md, not here.

## Brand Personality

Plain, exact, unflinching. The voice of a careful analyst who states a finding, gives its number, and names its limit in the same breath. Confidence comes from evidence and honest caveats, not from adjectives. The emotional goal is quiet alarm: the reader should feel the reach line being crossed.

## Anti-references

- Slide decks: one idea per full-screen panel, uniform section heights, arrow-key paging.
- SaaS landing pages: stat-card grids, hero metrics, gradient heroes, startup-marketing rhythm.
- Dashboards that show everything; this page shows only what carries the argument.

## Design Principles

1. One argument, in order. Every section must advance thesis, mechanism, model, limits or action; anything else is cut or linked.
2. The reach line is the signature. The orange line separating "in reach" from "out of reach" is the recurring visual idea, and orange means that and nothing else.
3. Technical where it counts. Say exactly what the model is (target, features, algorithm, split, test) and show it with charts. No separate limitations section; a caveat appears only as a short phrase where a number would otherwise mislead.
4. Show the real artifacts. Use the atlas and maps the team built rather than decorative illustration.
5. Flow, not slides. Sections vary in height and density and run into each other; motion is scroll-driven and always has a reduced-motion fallback.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA contrast. Full keyboard access and visible focus. All motion disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Charts carry text values so nothing depends on color alone; orange is never the only signal.
