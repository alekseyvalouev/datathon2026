San Francisco Bay Area · housing & migration · 2011–2026

Follow the people, not the place.

When a household leaves the Bay, two things happen that a regional average can't show. Its home is re-priced for a smaller, better-paid household that employers recruit in, and the household that left trades rent for burden, long commutes and lower pay somewhere else. In the averages, leavers and arrivals largely cancel out, so both stories disappear. Tracking people, units and flows brings them back.

Following the region

The average hides two moves that cancel out

Between 2011 and 2021, average income per tax return in the nine Bay counties rose from $101k to $213k. We used IRS migration records to split that rise into three parts: income growth among people who stayed, the effect of removing leavers, and the effect of adding arrivals.

Leavers usually had lower incomes than stayers, so removing them pushed the average up in 8 of 9 counties. Arrivals reported even less per return, so adding them pulled it back down. Region-wide, the two moves net to −8% of the rise. A county average reports that net and hides how large each move is.

Following the home

Each turnover re-prices the home for someone else

Zillow's price histories let us follow the same rental unit across tenants. We built 23,306 pairs of consecutive listings on the same unit. When a tenant leaves, the next asking rent jumps, by the most in San Francisco and northern San Mateo County.

California's AB 1482 caps a sitting tenant's increase at 5% plus inflation, with a 10% maximum. The cap ends when the tenant leaves, so the size of the jump is what a household pays to rent back in. In 2026, 23% of turnovers rose faster than 10% a year. After subtracting move-in deals like "4 weeks free," about 83% of the premium is still there.

San Francisco loses low-income units almost one-way. 20.7% of turnovers priced the unit out of low-income reach and only 0.7% brought one into reach. In 2026 the out-of-reach share was 28.7%.

Studios and one-bedrooms lose reach most often. Of rentals that were sold within two years, the buyer needs about 1.7× the income of the last renter, plus a $195k down payment.

Following the newcomer

Arrivals are smaller households, and employers help bring them in

IRS data seems to show arrivals as poorer than leavers: $144k vs $210k per tax return region-wide in 2021. Most of that gap comes from household size. Arrivals file smaller returns and are younger, and their incomes rise faster after the move.

Per person, the gap shrinks from 69% to 80% of leavers' income. Statewide, people moving into California file before age 35 at twice the rate of non-movers. Arrivals tend to be single renters who can afford the re-priced unit, and employers pay to bring many of them in.

Following the leaver

Leavers trade rent for burden, distance and lower pay

The California Policy Lab finds that 75% of low-income movers stay in the Bay and 8% go to the Central Valley. Census microdata follows people who lived in the Bay a year earlier. We compared those who left with stayers who match them on age, education, race, sex, household type and origin county.

Central Valley leavers are the only group more likely to own a home. They are also much more likely to pay half their income in rent, to commute an hour or more, and to earn less. Many keep their Bay jobs: 41,799 Bay jobs are held by San Joaquin County residents, up from 25,088 in 2010.

Following the data

The people worst off are the hardest to see

Stories about leaving the Bay online describe a different move from the one low-income families make. Each of our three predictive models was also weakest on the most vulnerable group.

Rent-jump model

Ridge · MAE 0.049 vs 0.059 baseline · AUC 0.82

Under-predicts jumps most in gentrifying tracts (−3.7 pts vs −2.1 in stable tracts). Adding a recent-trend feature narrows the gap but does not close it.

E2 · test set: 9,159 relistings in 2026

Who-leaves model

Gradient boosting · AUC 0.71 vs 0.50 base rate

Among the top 10% of risk scores it catches 41% of White leavers but only 25–28% of Asian, Black and Latino leavers. 36% of Central Valley leavers score below the median.

E9 · test set: 40,846 people

Service-desert model

Zillow features · AUC 0.92 vs 0.83 rule

Finds 69% of deserts among the most expensive rentals but only 31% among the cheapest, the units low-income households can actually rent.

E8 · test set: held-out map blocks

A tool built to target help would miss the people this page is about. Every model above should report results by group and be calibrated on recent data before it is used.
