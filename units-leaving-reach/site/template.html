What counts as out of reach

A unit is in reach if a household earning 80% of the county's area median income, sized to the unit (bedrooms + 1 people), qualifies under the common rule that income must be 3× the rent. It leaves reach when the next tenancy's settled asking rent is above that line. In San Francisco in 2026 the line for a 1-bedroom was $3,736.

How the projection works

  • A LightGBM classifier trained on 12,011 observed turnovers scores each unit's chance of leaving reach if it turns over in a given year. On held-out 2026 re-listings it scored AUC 0.90, against 0.85 for a rule based on the legal 10% rent-increase cap.
  • Chance out of reach by year Y = 1 − ∏ (1 − turnover rate × that year's score), using the turnover-rate slider.
  • Income limits grow at each county's 2022–26 rate (capped at 6% a year). County rents either keep their trailing 2026 trend or freeze.

Read with care

  • Dots are the 9,404 units Zillow showed listed for rent at least twice, rounded to about 100 m. Units that never return to market are not here.
  • On 2026 data the model under-predicted for apartments, renter-majority tracts and large landlords, so projections there are likely low.
  • Each year is scored from the unit's last observed rent. A unit that turns over and stays in reach would start from a new rent, which the projection ignores. Treat the maps as scenarios, not forecasts for any single building.
Data: Zillow property extract (Bright Data, The Bright Initiative), CA HCD State Income Limits 2022–2026, Urban Displacement Project tract typology, US Census boundaries. Model and code: datathon2026/units-leaving-reach.
