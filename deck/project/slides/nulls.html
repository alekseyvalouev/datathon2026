What didn’t work, stated as plainly as what did

Joining the workstreams barely helped

Tract and city-job features add +0.004 AUC across 10 seeds, and which one gets the credit isn’t stable. Distance to the line does most of the work.

Scores rank well but aren’t probabilities

In 2026 the riskiest tenth was predicted at 62% and observed at 81%. Use the score to order a list, not as a forecast.

Outer counties favor the simple rule

Trained without the county, the model loses to the legal-cap rule in Contra Costa (−0.014) and Sonoma (−0.017).

Asking rents, not signed leases

Some listings are floor plans, not units. Dropping implausible jumps over 30% a year keeps the gain (+0.039).

Limits
We want to be as clear about limits as about results. Combining the workstreams added very little to the model: most of its power is simple arithmetic about how close a unit sits to the affordability line. Its scores rank units well but under-state probabilities in 2026, so use it as a ranking. In two outer counties, a one-line rule does as well. And the data is asking rents, with some listings standing for floor plans; removing suspicious jumps doesn’t change the result.
