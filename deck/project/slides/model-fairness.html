Why

Ranking is even across groups (AUC 0.88–0.92). But 2026 rents in apartments and large-landlord buildings jumped past anything in training, and the model predicts about half the real rate there.

A policy choice, not a fix

A separate top-20% list for each tract type raises low-income-tract recall from 0.62 to 0.76, and costs 5 points of regional recall (0.72 to 0.67).

Model: who it misses
Here’s who the model misses. It ranks equally well within every group, but when you flag a fixed 20 percent of units, it catches fewer of the losses in low-income tracts, renter tracts and apartments than in wealthy, owner and house-heavy areas. The reason is that 2026 rents in apartments and big-landlord buildings rose faster than anything it trained on, so it under-predicts there. It still beats the rule in every group. If you give each tract type its own list, low-income recall jumps to 0.76 at a small regional cost. That’s a choice for whoever uses it, and we show the trade-off rather than hide it.
