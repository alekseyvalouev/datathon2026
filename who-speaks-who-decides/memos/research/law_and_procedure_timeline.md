## Key question 1: State laws - what changed and when (effective dates)

### Takeaway
California's state changes cluster on 1 January of each year, with the strongest hearing-relevant breaks at 2018-01-01 (HAA standard of review plus SB 35), 2020-01-01 (SB 330 five-hearing cap), 2023-07-01 (AB 2011), 2024-06-28 (San Francisco made subject to SB 423 at a 10% affordability threshold, which removes most code-compliant housing from Planning Commission approval hearings), and 2025-01-01 (AB 1893). Several effective dates the team asked for (AB 1633, AB 2345, AB 1287, the 2016 and 2019 ADU packages, SB 35 statutory effective date) were not confirmed on a page in this session and are in Gaps.

### Cited Findings

**Housing Accountability Act (Gov. Code 65589.5)**
- SB 167 / AB 678 / AB 1515 were signed by Gov. Brown on 29 September 2017 and took effect 1 January 2018. — [Holland & Knight, Sept 29 2017](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2017/09/california-governor-signs-into-law-major-reforms-t)
- These bills raised the local agency's burden from substantial evidence to preponderance of the evidence when it denies or downsizes a housing project; a project is deemed consistent with objective standards if substantial evidence would let a reasonable person conclude so (AB 1515); the agency must give written notice of any inconsistency within a statutory deadline or the project is deemed consistent; courts must fine non-complying agencies at least $10,000 per unit, multiplied by five for bad faith. — [Holland & Knight, Sept 29 2017](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2017/09/california-governor-signs-into-law-major-reforms-t)
  - Applies to: all housing development projects (not only affordable). Expected effect: fewer outright denials and fewer density-reduction conditions after 2018-01-01.
- AB 3194 (Daly) took effect 1 January 2019: a project that complies with objective general plan standards cannot be found inconsistent with zoning or made to rezone where zoning conflicts with the general plan; legislative intent that "specific, adverse impact" on health and safety findings arise infrequently. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2018](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2018/10/californias-2019-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-kno)
  - Expected effect: small; marginally fewer denials.
- AB 1633 (2023) amended the HAA so that certain CEQA process failures count as a disapproval, but only for dense housing on a narrow class of infill sites. — [Elmendorf, NYU Envtl. L.J. 2024](https://nyuelj.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Elmendorf-Lawyering-Cities-Into-Housing-Shortages.pdf); [SPUR California Housing Laws guide, 2026](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/SPUR_California_Housing_Laws.pdf)
  - Expected effect: fewer CEQA-driven continuances and CEQA appeals for infill housing (mostly a Board of Supervisors appeal effect, weak at Planning Commission). Effective date not confirmed in session (see Gaps).
- AB 1893 took effect 1 January 2025 (the Holland & Knight summary says laws take effect then unless noted and does not flag AB 1893 as an exception). It makes it an HAA violation to subject an HAA-protected project to more than five hearings including continuances, expands HAA coverage to certain mixed-use projects, raises fines for repeat violators, and reworks the builder's remedy. — [Holland & Knight, Nov 2024](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/11/californias-2025-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know); [Cox Castle on AB 1893](https://www.coxcastle.com/publication-ab-1893-expands-the-housing-accountability-acts-protections) (Cox Castle page appeared in search results but was not opened)
  - Expected effect: fewer continuances per housing item from 2025-01-01, because continuances now count against the five-hearing cap and exceeding it is itself an HAA violation.

**SB 35 (2017) and SB 423 (2023) ministerial streamlining**
- San Francisco began implementing SB 35 in January 2018; at that time only projects with more than 50% of units affordable qualified. — [SF Planning, SB 423 page](https://sfplanning.org/node/996)
- SB 765 (effective 1 January 2019) clarified that CEQA does not apply to the determination of SB 35 eligibility. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2018](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2018/10/californias-2019-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-kno)
- AB 1174 (urgency, effective 17 September 2021) and AB 2668 (1 January 2023, inferred from the general rule) made further SB 35 technical amendments. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2021](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2021/10/californias-2022-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know); [Holland & Knight, Oct 2022](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/california-2023-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)
- SB 423 passed in the 2023 session and amended and extended SB 35; SF Planning said it would process SB 423 applications "starting in early 2024". — [SF Planning, SB 423 page](https://sfplanning.org/node/996)
- SB 423 requires San Francisco specifically to demonstrate housing production progress annually. HCD's determination of 28 June 2024 changed the San Francisco eligibility threshold from 50% of units affordable at 80% AMI to 10% of units affordable at 50% AMI (local inclusionary still applies if higher). Qualifying: code-compliant projects of 2-9 units, and code-compliant projects of 10+ units meeting SF inclusionary requirements; State Density Bonus projects count as code-compliant. Excluded: projects that demolish tenant-occupied or rent-controlled units or listed historic structures. Planning staff approve ministerially with no Planning Commission or Board approval hearing; timelines are 90 days (150 units or fewer) and 180 days (more than 150 units). — [Coblentz Patch Duffy & Bass, 2024](https://www.coblentzlaw.com/?p=9000)
- Press coverage on 1 July 2024 described the law as taking effect in San Francisco that day; other California cities do not face the equivalent review until 2026; most projects no longer go to the Planning Commission and cannot be appealed to the Board; projects under development agreements (e.g. Stonestown) remain discretionary; approval time expected to fall from over two years to about six months. — [SFist, July 1 2024](https://sfist.com/2024/07/01/sf-forced-by-state-to-streamline-housing-approvals-after-missing-2023-production-goals)
- A residual hearing remains: for sites in moderate-resource, low-resource, or high-segregation-and-poverty areas on the CTCAC/HCD Opportunity Map, a Planning Commission informational hearing is held within 45 days of the Notice of Intent, before submittal; the public can comment but the sponsor need not change the project. — [SF Planning, State Housing Legislation slides](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/State_Housing_Legislation.pdf)
  - Expected effect from 2024-06-28: sharp drop in housing CU / LPA / DR items; a new item type (SB 423 informational hearings, no action taken) appears; public comment per housing project falls because most projects have no hearing. Note the conflict between "June 28" (HCD determination, Coblentz) and "July 1" (press); treat the window 2024-06-28 to 2024-07-01 as the break.

**SB 330 Housing Crisis Act and the five-hearing limit**
- SB 330 was signed 9 October 2019 and took effect 1 January 2020. — [SCAG summary of SB 330 and SB 8](https://scag.ca.gov/sites/main/files/file-attachments/senate_bill_330_and_senate_bill_8_-_summary_of_housing_crisis_act_of_2019.pdf)
- Gov. Code 65905.5: a housing development project that complies with objective general plan and zoning standards in effect when the application is deemed complete may be subject to at most five hearings. "Hearing" includes any public hearing, workshop or similar meeting, including appeals. Hearings on legislative approvals (general plan amendments, specific plans, zoning amendments) do not count. — [SCAG summary](https://scag.ca.gov/sites/main/files/file-attachments/senate_bill_330_and_senate_bill_8_-_summary_of_housing_crisis_act_of_2019.pdf)
- SB 330 also created the preliminary application (Gov. Code 65941.1) that freezes applicable standards and fees at submittal, with a 180-day deadline to file the full application, and froze zoning at 1 January 2018 levels for downzoning purposes (Gov. Code 66300). — [SCAG summary](https://scag.ca.gov/sites/main/files/file-attachments/senate_bill_330_and_senate_bill_8_-_summary_of_housing_crisis_act_of_2019.pdf)
- SB 8 was signed 16 September 2021 and extended the Housing Crisis Act sunset from 1 January 2025 to 1 January 2030; it also made meetings related to State Density Bonus Law count toward the five-hearing cap. — [SCAG summary](https://scag.ca.gov/sites/main/files/file-attachments/senate_bill_330_and_senate_bill_8_-_summary_of_housing_crisis_act_of_2019.pdf)
- SB 8 took effect 1 January 2022 (general rule for 2021 bills). Holland & Knight describes the five-hearing limit as running "through 2034" and vesting for preliminary applications filed before 1 January 2030 as lasting until 1 January 2034. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2021](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2021/10/californias-2022-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know); the SCAG summary gives only the 2030 sunset — [SCAG](https://scag.ca.gov/sites/main/files/file-attachments/senate_bill_330_and_senate_bill_8_-_summary_of_housing_crisis_act_of_2019.pdf). The two sources differ on how long the hearing cap runs (2030 vs 2034); this does not matter for a dataset ending March 2026.
- AB 130 (2025) amended SB 330 and made it permanent, and added CEQA exemptions and new approval deadlines; SB 131 (FY 2025-26 budget trailer bill) exempted urban housing and certain infill from lengthy CEQA review. — [SPUR California Housing Laws guide, 2026](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/SPUR_California_Housing_Laws.pdf)
  - Expected effect of SB 330 from 2020-01-01: the distribution of hearings-per-housing-case should be truncated (fewer cases with 4+ appearances), fewer serial continuances for code-compliant housing. Confounded by the pandemic starting ten weeks later.

**State Density Bonus Law**
- AB 2345 raised the maximum State Density Bonus from 35% to 50%. — [SPUR guide](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/SPUR_California_Housing_Laws.pdf)
- AB 1287 added further bonus levels for very-low or moderate-income units (stacking) and up to five concessions; SF Planning said it would update its bulletin and applications. — [SPUR guide](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/SPUR_California_Housing_Laws.pdf); [SF Planning, State Housing Legislation slides](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/State_Housing_Legislation.pdf)
- 2019-effective density bonus bills: AB 2753, AB 2372, SB 1227 (student housing), AB 2797. 2022-effective: SB 290, SB 728, AB 571. 2023-effective: AB 2334 (base density, 100% affordable in low-VMT areas), AB 1551. 2025-effective: AB 2694, AB 3116. — [H&K 2019](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2018/10/californias-2019-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-kno); [H&K 2022](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2021/10/californias-2022-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know); [H&K 2023](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/california-2023-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know); [H&K 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/11/californias-2025-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)
  - Expected effect: more items whose description mentions "State Density Bonus"; density bonus projects are harder to deny or reshape, so fewer conditions and continuances. Effective dates for AB 2345 and AB 1287 not confirmed in session (Gaps).

**AB 2011 and SB 6**
- Both took effect 1 July 2023. AB 2011 gives a ministerial, CEQA-exempt path for qualifying multifamily projects on commercially zoned land with prevailing wage and affordability; SB 6 allows residential use on commercially zoned property without rezoning (the source does not say SB 6 removes hearings). — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2022](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/california-2023-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)
- In San Francisco, AB 2011 projects need no CUA or LPA and no CEQA; review timelines are 90 days (150 units or fewer) and 180 days (more than 150). — [SF Planning, State Housing Legislation slides](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/State_Housing_Legislation.pdf)
  - Expected effect: modest removal of commercial-corridor housing items from the calendar from 2023-07-01.

**Other state laws with verified dates**
- SB 9 (ministerial duplexes and lot splits), SB 10, SB 478: effective 1 January 2022. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2021](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2021/10/californias-2022-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)
- AB 2097 (no minimum parking within a half mile of transit): effective 1 January 2023. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2022](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/california-2023-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)
- AB 2234 (post-entitlement permit deadlines), AB 2221 and SB 897 (ADU clean-up, 60-day action, height), AB 916 (no hearing to add up to two bedrooms inside existing space): effective 1 January 2023. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2022](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2022/10/california-2023-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)
- AB 1114 defines post-entitlement phase permits for San Francisco, sets review timelines and prohibits appeals of those permits; SF staff were preparing to implement it by 1 January 2024. — [SF Planning slides](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/State_Housing_Legislation.pdf); [sf.gov, Dec 2023](https://www.sf.gov/news/mayor-breed-signs-new-major-housing-law-sets-next-steps-housing-agenda)
- SB 4 (ministerial housing on land owned by religious and higher-education institutions) is processed by SF Planning with no CEQA, CUA or LPA. — [SF Planning slides](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/State_Housing_Legislation.pdf)
- SB 1333 (effective 1 January 2019) made charter cities such as San Francisco subject to ADU permitting laws that previously applied only to general law cities. — [Holland & Knight, Oct 2018](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2018/10/californias-2019-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-kno)
- 2025-effective: SB 1211 (up to eight detached ADUs on multifamily lots), SB 951 (bars appeals of San Francisco coastal development permits to the Coastal Commission), both 1 January 2025. — [Holland & Knight, Nov 2024](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/11/californias-2025-housing-laws-what-you-need-to-know)
- SB 79 (transit-oriented housing as an allowed use near transit, with minimum densities and heights) is listed in SPUR's 2026 guide. — [SPUR guide](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/SPUR_California_Housing_Laws.pdf)

### Inferences
- AB 2097 and SB 9 are unlikely to show in SF Planning Commission data: San Francisco had already eliminated minimum parking citywide before 2023 (not verified in session), and SB 9 projects are ministerial and small. Treat both as placebo dates.
- ADU laws mainly move work off the Commission calendar (ministerial, 60-day approval); any effect would appear as fewer small residential CU / DR items, not as a change in comments.
- The 2018-01-01 break combines two things (HAA burden shift and SB 35); they cannot be separated by date alone. SB 35 in 2018-2024 only touched projects at least 50% affordable, so its volume effect before 2024-06-28 should be small.
- The 2020-01-01 SB 330 break is only ten weeks before the pandemic interruption; an ITS on hearings-per-case needs to use case cohorts (cases filed or deemed complete after 2020-01-01) rather than calendar time.
- The 2024-06-28 SB 423 trigger is probably the single largest state-driven break for "which projects need a hearing at all".

## Key question 2: San Francisco ordinances and policies

### Takeaway
The local changes with the clearest, verified effective dates and direct hearing consequences are: Section 317 expansion (2016-04-10, adds hearings), HOME-SF (2017-07-13), the Process Improvements Ordinance (2018-08-26, with notice changes operative 2019-01-01, removes hearings and DR for affordable housing), the Constraints Reduction Ordinance (signed 2023-12-14, removes CU and 311 notice for much code-compliant housing outside Priority Equity Geographies), the Family Zoning Plan (effective 2026-01-12), and the Tenant Protections Ordinance (effective 2026-02-09). Inclusionary-rate changes (2016, 2017, 2023, 2026) mainly change project feasibility and pipeline timing rather than hearing rules.

### Cited Findings

**Discretionary Review (DR) reform, 2009-2010**
- The Planning Commission adopted the DR Reform Proposal (a Commission policy plus accompanying legislation) on 18 June 2009. Content: strengthened and expanded pre-application meeting requirements, mandatory Residential Design Team review, a definition of "exceptional and extraordinary" circumstances, only projects meeting that standard to go to a Commission hearing, a 90-day cap on DR processing, and a two-year trial period. — [SF Planning DR reform web summary, April 2010](https://default.sfplanning.org/NP/WebsiteSummary4_13_10.pdf)
- The Department implemented the non-legislative elements after June 2009 and reported fewer DR applications. The legislation (which would have delegated DR authority) was amended and continued to the Call of the Chair by the Board's Land Use Committee after five hearings through 8 March 2010; members said more public trust was needed before delegating DR authority. — [SF Planning DR reform web summary, April 2010](https://default.sfplanning.org/NP/WebsiteSummary4_13_10.pdf)
  - Applies to: building permits in residential districts subject to Section 311 notice. Expected effect: a gradual decline in DR filings from mid-2009 (policy-level, not a sharp legal break); DR hearings did NOT get delegated to staff.
- Background: DR in San Francisco traces to a 1943 California Supreme Court case and a 1954 City Attorney opinion grounding it in the municipal code; the scholarly account argues its legal basis is contested. — [Elmendorf, NYU Envtl. L.J. 2024](https://nyuelj.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Elmendorf-Lawyering-Cities-Into-Housing-Shortages.pdf)

**Section 317 dwelling-unit removal, 2016**
- Ordinance 23-16 (Board File 150494), mainly C-3 districts, effective 3 April 2016 (the page header says April 4; the body says April 3); Ordinance 33-16 (Board File 160115), rest of the city, effective 10 April 2016 and partly superseding the first. — [SF Planning code summary 160115/150494](https://SFPlanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/code-summaries/160115_150494.pdf)
- Before: outside RTO / NCT / Upper Market NCD and ground-floor C-3, loss of three or more units needed Conditional Use; loss of one or two units needed Mandatory Discretionary Review; removal of unauthorized units in buildings with three or more legal units needed Mandatory DR (January 2014 Mayor's directive), and in one- or two-unit buildings was approved over the counter. After: Conditional Use is required in all districts for loss of any residential unit by demolition, merger or conversion, and for removal of unauthorized units; administrative approval of mergers eliminated. — [SF Planning code summary](https://SFPlanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/code-summaries/160115_150494.pdf)
- Transition: unit removals already approved before 10 April 2016 are exempt; unauthorized-unit rules apply to building permits issued on or after 1 March 2016; DBI can screen out units that cannot be legalized. — [SF Planning code summary](https://SFPlanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/code-summaries/160115_150494.pdf); corroborated by [Costa-Hawkins.com, April 2016](https://costa-hawkins.com/surreal-estate/san-francisco-legislative-update-2016-conditional-use-approval-required-for-removal-of-authorized-and-unauthorized-units/)
  - Expected effect from 2016-04-10: jump in CU items for demolition / merger / unauthorized-unit removal; corresponding fall in Mandatory DR items (reclassification from DRM to CUA case suffixes); CU items carry more formal findings and likely more denials than DRs.
- An earlier Section 317 ordinance exists (code summary 080210, "Loss of Dwelling Units", 2008) and a 2013 ordinance (Ord. 287-13); both surfaced in search results but were not opened. — [SF Planning code summary 080210](https://SFPlanning.org/sites/default/files/documents/legis/code-summaries/080210_Loss_of_DU-PC_317.pdf)

**Inclusionary housing rates**
- History per SF Planning: 2002 Inclusionary Housing Ordinance at 10%; 2007 raised to 15% on-site; 2012 charter amendment (Housing Trust Fund) lowered on-site to 12% and froze changes; June 2016 Prop C restored the Board's ability to set rates; a May 2016 ordinance raised requirements pending further action and grandfathered lower rates for pipeline projects; August 2017 ordinance set on-site rates at 18% rental and 20% condo, rising annually toward 24% and 26%, with a lower rate for 10-24 unit projects. — [SF Planning, Inclusionary Affordable Housing Program](https://sfplanning.org/inclusionary-affordable-housing-program)
- Ordinance 187-23 (dated 5 September 2023 on the SF Planning page): temporary on-site rate of 12% for pipeline projects approved before 1 November 2023 and 15% for projects approved after, plus a temporary 33% reduction in Article 4 impact fees, set to expire 1 November 2026. — [SF Planning, Inclusionary Affordable Housing Program](https://sfplanning.org/inclusionary-affordable-housing-program). A law-firm summary describes Ordinance 187-23 as passed in July 2023. — [JD Supra / Coblentz](https://jdsupra.com/legalnews/approved-san-francisco-legislation-3605479)
  - Expected effect: a rush of approvals and of modification requests for already-entitled projects ahead of the 1 November 2023 pipeline cutoff (look for a spike in items in September-October 2023).
- 30 April 2026: Controller's triennial review recommended 5% on-site (and, per the ordinance findings, fee and off-site rates cut from about 20-20.5% to 10%), contingent on a stable alternative funding source. — [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/inclusionary-affordable-housing-program); [Ordinance 136-26 text](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/o0136-26.pdf)
- Ordinance 136-26 (Board File 260538, "Inclusionary Housing Program Updates and Reductions; Development Impact Fee Reductions"; sponsors Mayor Lurie and Supervisors Melgar, Dorsey, Sherrill, Sauter, Mandelman): Planning Commission Resolution 21923 on 18 June 2026; amended at the Board 14 July 2026; approved by the Board 21 July 2026; effective 22 August 2026; Planning began accepting pipeline modification requests 24 August 2026. It reduces requirements for projects of 25+ units and removes them for projects of 24 or fewer units. ## Key question 3: Public-comment procedure at the SF Planning Commission

### Takeaway
Three procedural regimes are confirmed: remote-only hearings by 16 April 2020; hybrid hearings (in person at City Hall plus phone / WebEx comment) by 28 April 2022 and still in place on 5 October 2023; and in-person-only public comment from 1 January 2024, when the Planning Commission and Historic Preservation Commission suspended remote comment. The standard speaker limit was three minutes throughout, so comment-count changes are not explained by a rule change in per-speaker time.

### Cited Findings
- The 16 April 2020 agenda is headed "Remote Hearing" via video and teleconference, citing the Governor's shelter-in-place order, and says the Commission "was authorized to resume their hearing schedule through May 3, 2020 remotely"; public comment was by toll-free call-in. The same agenda shows items continued "without hearing" on 19 March 2020 to 26 March, then to 9 April, then 16 April 2020, i.e. hearings in the second half of March 2020 did not take place. — [SF Planning agenda, April 16 2020](https://sf-planning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2020-04/20200416_cal.pdf)
- The notice for 28 April 2022 is titled a hybrid hearing: held in person in Commission Chambers, Room 400, City Hall, with a public-comment call-in number retained; some commissioners could attend by video under Gov. Code 54953(e) and the Mayor's 45th supplement to the 25 February 2020 emergency proclamation. — [Planning Commission notice of hybrid hearing, April 28 2022](https://readkong.com/page/san-francisco-planning-commission-notice-of-hybrid-2833338)
- On 5 October 2023 the Commission was still hybrid: in person in Room 400 with remote comment by phone ((415) 655-0001 with a per-hearing access code, "press *3 to raise your hand") and by WebEx. — [SF Planning agenda, Oct 5 2023](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2023-09/20231005_cpc%20%28ID%201391569%29.pdf)
- The Planning Commission and Historic Preservation Commission "have suspended remote public comment for hearings as of January 1, 2024"; remote comment continues only at select Environmental Review Officer and Zoning Administrator hearings. — [SF Planning, Remote Public Comment](https://sfplanning.org/remotehearings)
- Context: the Board of Supervisors voted 8-3 on 17 October 2023 to end remote public comment at Board and committee meetings (with some exceptions) after racist and antisemitic calls disrupted its 26 September 2023 meeting. That action covered the Board, not the Planning Commission. — [Local News Matters / Bay City News, Oct 19 2023](https://localnewsmatters.org/2023/10/19/sf-supervisors-abolish-remote-comments-at-meetings-following-wave-of-extremist-calls/)
- Speaker time limits (identical in the April 2020, April 2022 and October 2023 agendas): general public comment up to three minutes per person, and may be moved to the end of the agenda if it exceeds 15 minutes in total; on agenda items, individual proponents and opponents up to three minutes each; project sponsor up to 10 minutes (15 with a written request 72 hours ahead); organized opposition up to 10 minutes with at least three speakers; in Discretionary Review cases the requestor gets five minutes, the sponsor five (up to 10 with multiple requestors), supporters three each, and two minutes rebuttal per side; the President may impose additional limits. — [April 2020 agenda](https://sf-planning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2020-04/20200416_cal.pdf); [April 2022 notice](https://readkong.com/page/san-francisco-planning-commission-notice-of-hybrid-2833338); [Oct 2023 agenda](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2023-09/20231005_cpc%20%28ID%201391569%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- Expected signatures: (a) March 2020: a gap of roughly three to four weeks with no hearings, then remote-only; comment counts per item likely rose in the remote / hybrid period because calling in is cheap; (b) spring 2022: mixed in-person and remote; (c) from the first hearing of January 2024: comment counts per item should fall, and the speaker mix should shift toward people able to attend a Thursday 1 p.m. hearing at City Hall. The January 2024 change is citywide and item-type-neutral, which distinguishes it from the Constraints Reduction Ordinance.
- Because the President can shorten the per-speaker limit on crowded items (commonly to one or two minutes), speaker COUNT is a more stable outcome than total minutes; if the dataset has comment text length, expect shorter comments on high-volume items regardless of period.
- If the dataset records whether a comment was by phone / WebEx or in person, the remote share from April 2022 to December 2023 gives a direct estimate of how many comments were lost in 2024.

## Key question 4: Market context (controls)

### Takeaway
Permit volume is the main confounder: San Francisco permitted 779 units in 2010, peaked at about 5,200 in 2018, and fell to 1,136 in 2023, the lowest since 2010, which sources attribute to interest rates, construction costs and flat rents. Hearing counts will follow this cycle with a lag regardless of legal changes, so the ITS should normalise by application volume or include permits as a covariate.

### Cited Findings
- HUD building-permit counts for San Francisco as reported in the trade press: 2010 = 779 units; 2018 = 5,200 units (highest year cited); 2023 = 1,136 units, the lowest since 2010 (1,076 of them in buildings of five or more units); 2001-2023 average about 2,476 per year. — [The Real Deal, Feb 7 2024](https://therealdeal.com/sanfrancisco/2024/02/07/sf-building-permits-for-housing-slumps-to-13-year-low)
- Causes cited for the 2023 slump: Federal Reserve rate increases raising construction financing costs, post-pandemic construction cost inflation and supply-chain shortages, and a Controller's Office finding that city requirements, rising costs and flat rents made multifamily housing hard to finance. — [The Real Deal, Feb 7 2024](https://therealdeal.com/sanfrancisco/2024/02/07/sf-building-permits-for-housing-slumps-to-13-year-low)
- Before SB 423 applied, average approval time for housing in San Francisco was reported as more than two years. — [SFist, July 1 2024](https://sfist.com/2024/07/01/sf-forced-by-state-to-streamline-housing-approvals-after-missing-2023-production-goals)
- Data sources for a year-by-year or monthly control series (surfaced, not opened): FRED series BPPRIV006075, "New Private Housing Structures Authorized by Building Permits for San Francisco County" — [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/BPPRIV006075.txt); SF Planning annual Housing Inventory reports — [2023 Housing Inventory](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/resources/2024-04/2023_Housing_Inventory.pdf), [2024 Housing Inventory](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/resources/2025-04/2024_Housing%20Inventory.pdf); MTC Vital Signs housing permits — [MTC](https://vitalsigns.mtc.ca.gov/indicators/housing-permits).

### Inferences
- Approximate regime dating for controls (my synthesis, partly from the figures above and partly general knowledge): 2008-2011 financial-crisis trough (2010 low of 779 units); 2012-2019 expansion with a 2018 peak; March 2020 pandemic shock; 2022-2024 interest-rate slump with the 2023 trough. The Federal Reserve's rate increases began in March 2022 (general knowledge, not sourced here).
- Entitlement hearings lead building permits (projects are heard before they pull permits), so the hearing series should turn down earlier than permits: expect housing entitlement items to peak around 2015-2017 and to thin from 2019-2020.
- The 2023-12 / 2024-01 / 2024-06 legal breaks all fall inside the market trough. A fall in housing items in 2024 could be either "fewer projects need a hearing" or "fewer projects exist". Non-housing items (cannabis, formula retail, office allocation) and housing applications filed (from the SF Planning permit / PPTS dataset on DataSF) are the natural controls.

### Gaps
- Year-by-year permit and entitlement counts for 2005-2025 were not extracted; only 2010, 2018 and 2023 are sourced. Pull the FRED or Housing Inventory series directly.
- No sourced figures for 2024 or 2025 permits, or for the count of projects approved under SB 423 since June 2024 (a Senator Wiener press release on falling approval times surfaced at https://sd11.senate.ca.gov/news/san-francisco-housing-approval-times-plummet-senator-scott-wieners-landmark-housing-law-takes but was not opened).
- No sourced data on entitlement application volume (as distinct from building permits) by year.
