Researcher note on method and limits: the sandbox could not reach `data.sfgov.org`, `netfile.com`, `data.stanford.edu`, `fppc.ca.gov/transparency/...` or GitHub raw/API directly (proxy/allowlist blocks), so no dataset was actually downloaded or row-counted. ## 1. San Francisco Ethics Commission data on DataSF (dataset names/IDs, IE coverage, supported/opposed candidate, dates)

### Takeaway
San Francisco is the only Bay Area jurisdiction confirmed to offer a clean, nightly-updated, public-domain (PDDL) Socrata transactions table that includes Form 496 (24-hour IE) and Form 460 Schedule D rows; it was last updated 11 Nov 2026, so Nov 3, 2026 election filings are already in it. SF alone gives roughly 10-13 candidate contests plus 12-17 measures per November election, which is small for causal inference unless pooled across cycles or analysed at precinct level.

### Cited Findings
- Core DataSF datasets and IDs listed by the Ethics Commission: Campaign Filers `4c8t-ngau`; Campaign Filings Received `qizs-bwft`; Summary Totals `9ggq-m8hp`; Transactions `pitq-e56w`; Public Financing Disbursements `dbak-p2fq`. — [SF Ethics: Campaign Finance Disclosure Data](https://sfethics.org/disclosures/campaign-finance-disclosure/campaign-finance-disclosure-data)
- Local-law datasets also on DataSF: Form 107 `i2q2-jqhq`, Form 113 `48rt-5dkm`, Form 122 `i7fj-76x7`, Form 128 (acceptance of expenditure ceiling) `h8vs-2sf8`, Form 134b (notice of exceeding 100% of voluntary expenditure ceiling) `r6ws-g6g2`, Form 143 `5cap-nnzt`. — [SF Ethics: Campaign Finance Disclosure Data](https://sfethics.org/disclosures/campaign-finance-disclosure/campaign-finance-disclosure-data)
- The Ethics Commission recommends the SODA (Socrata) API for these datasets. — [SF Ethics: Campaign Finance Disclosure Data](https://sfethics.org/disclosures/campaign-finance-disclosure/campaign-finance-disclosure-data)
- "Campaign Finance - Transactions" (`pitq-e56w`) contains transactions from FPPC Forms 460, 461, 496, 497 and 450, combining all schedules and including unitemized totals; only the most recent version (original/amendment) of each filing appears; rejected filings excluded. Form 460 schedules present: A, B1, B2, C, D (expenditures supporting/opposing other candidates, measures and committees), E (payments made), F, G, H, I. — [data.gov catalog entry](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/campaign-finance-transactions)

- Licence: PDDL 1.0 (public domain dedication). — [data.gov catalog entry](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/campaign-finance-transactions)
- Direct download URLs: CSV `https://data.sf.gov/api/v3/views/pitq-e56w/export.csv?accessType=DOWNLOAD`; JSON `https://data.sf.gov/api/v3/views/pitq-e56w/query.json?accessType=DOWNLOAD`; column dictionary `https://data.sf.gov/api/views/pitq-e56w/columns.json`. (Note the domain is now `data.sf.gov`.) — [data.gov catalog entry](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/campaign-finance-transactions)
- Catalog page mentions a "Form Type" column, "-nid" join keys, "Filing Id Number", "Filing Date" and "Filing Activity Nid" but does not enumerate all columns. — [data.gov catalog entry](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/campaign-finance-transactions)
- An older, separate DataSF dataset `p4sp-es3b` holds itemized contributions of $100+ from Form 496 Part 3, 2009 to present, in the CAL-format layout (~74 fields) including `Filer_ID`, `Filer_NamL`, `Cand_Nam*`, `Bal_Name`, `Bal_Num`, `Sup_Opp_Cd`, `Off_S_H_Cd`, `Dist_No`, `Juris_Dscr`, `Elect_Date`, `Tran_Date`, `Tran_Amt1`, `Tran_Amt2`, `Form_Type`, `Rec_Type`. - FPPC Forms 460, 461, 465, 496 and 497 are additionally available as exports from the NetFile public site for SF (`public.netfile.com/pub2/?aid=sfo`), with an export key linked from the Ethics page. — [SF Ethics: Campaign Finance Disclosure Data](https://sfethics.org/disclosures/campaign-finance-disclosure/campaign-finance-disclosure-data)

Form 460 data covers the election calendar year and the prior calendar year. — [About the dashboards](https://sfethics.org/disclosures/campaign-finance-disclosure/campaign-finance-dashboards/dashboards-about-the-dashboards)

Candidate contests listed: Public Defender; Board of Supervisors D02, D04, D06, D08, D10; Board of Education; Assessor-Recorder; Community College Board (plus a combined CCB/BoE line). Measures A-J (12 lines including combined "C,I" and "D,E,F"). Largest pot: Measures D,E,F committee $13.8M raised / $11.96M spent; Measure H $5.3M; Sup. D08 $1.77M funds / $2.09M expenses; Sup. D10 $1.45M / $1.32M. — [SF dashboard, Nov 3 2026](https://campaign.sfethics.org/elections/2026-11-03)

### Inferences
- The Transactions table should allow reconstruction of a dated IE series per supported/opposed candidate: filter `form_type` to F496 (and F460 Schedule D for IEs outside the 90-day window), group by support/oppose code + candidate/measure + transaction date. The existence of these fields in `pitq-e56w` is inferred from (a) the CAL layout shown for `p4sp-es3b` and (b) the dashboards deriving per-candidate third-party support/opposition from the same data; confirm via `columns.json` first.
- Expect double counting between Form 496 rows and later Form 460 Schedule D rows for the same spending; the dashboards handle this by only using 496s filed after the latest 460 deadline. The team needs the same rule (or use only 496 for timing, only 460-D for totals).
- Rough SF sample: about 13 candidate contests in a presidential-year November and about 9-10 in a midterm November; with roughly 2-10 candidates per contest, SF alone yields on the order of dozens (not hundreds) of candidate-races with IE activity per cycle. Supervisor districts (11 districts, about half up each cycle) are the natural unit. Precinct-level outcomes are the only way to get statistical power within SF.
- 2-day feasibility: HIGH. One CSV download (or SODA query with `$where` on form type and election date), no key needed for modest volumes.

## 2. California Secretary of State: Cal-Access raw export, California Civic Data Coalition, CARS status, and whether Cal-Access covers local races

### Takeaway
CARS has NOT launched: as of the SOS go-live page it is "In Development" with a target go-live of 16 February 2027, so the legacy `dbwebexport.zip` and URLs are unchanged in November 2026. Cal-Access is a state-level system; local candidate and local-measure committees file with city/county filing officers, so Cal-Access is of marginal use for this project.

### Cited Findings
- Raw export URL: `https://campaignfinance.cdn.sos.ca.gov/dbwebexport.zip`; documentation: `https://campaignfinance.cdn.sos.ca.gov/calaccess-documentation.zip`. Format: tab-delimited text files, one per CAL-ACCESS table, zipped; updated once per day; no technical support; unredacted addresses need a Data Processing Request. No licence text stated; no known outages reported. — [SOS: Raw Data for Campaign Finance and Lobbying Activity](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/helpful-resources/raw-data-campaign-finance-and-lobbying-activity)
- CARS status: "In Development", target go-live 16 February 2027 ("will be available for use in February, 2027"). Historical filing data will be migrated; after CARS becomes available the current CAL-ACCESS system "will no longer be available for use". The page says the public will search and filter data through the new interface using plain-language descriptions rather than the form-based structure; it says nothing about bulk export, API or the fate of `dbwebexport.zip`. — [SOS: CARS Go-Live Updates](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/helpful-resources/cal-access-replacement-system-project-cars-updates/cal-access-replacement-system-cars-go-live-updates)
- Background on delays: project initiated 2016, originally scheduled June 2021, paused; in April 2024 CalMatters reported it was "probably not before December 2026"; about $70M allocated and at least $40M spent as of Sept 2022; an independent assessment found the earlier build flawed at multiple layers. — [CalMatters, April 2024](https://calmatters.org/politics/2024/04/california-campaign-finance-website/)
- Under FPPC Manual 1 (state candidates), Form 496 for state contests is filed electronically with the Secretary of State with no copies to other filing officers. — [FPPC Manual 1, Ch. 12](https://fppc.ca.gov/content/dam/fppc/NS-Documents/TAD/Campaign%20Manuals/Manual_1/Manual-1-Chapter-12-Reporting-Independent-Expenditures.pdf)
- SF's own dashboards state that state filers are not included in local data and direct users to Cal-Access for them, i.e. the two systems are disjoint by filer type. — [About the SF dashboards](https://sfethics.org/disclosures/campaign-finance-disclosure/campaign-finance-dashboards/dashboards-about-the-dashboards)
- The SOS maintains a separate "City and County Electronic Filings" link directory pointing to each local agency's own site, and notes unlisted cities/counties "may still have filing requirements but do not provide online access". — [SOS: City and County Electronic Filings](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/cal-access-resources/city-and-county-electronic-filings)

### Inferences
- Because local filings sit with local filing officers and the SOS only links out to them, Cal-Access contains local-race money only incidentally: state-registered general-purpose committees (e.g. statewide PACs, unions, parties) that spend in local races report that on their own state-filed Form 460 Schedule D. That is a recipient-side-incomplete and messy route; do not rely on it in 2 days.
- No URL/format change affects a November 2026 project; the change risk begins February 2027.
- 2-day feasibility of Cal-Access for this question: LOW value (wrong level of government), MEDIUM effort (the zip is large; UNVERIFIED recollection is roughly 1 GB compressed / several GB unzipped with ~80 tables, key tables `EXPN_CD`, `S496_CD`, `RCPT_CD`, `CVR_CAMPAIGN_DISCLOSURE_CD`, `FILER_FILINGS_CD`).

### Gaps
- California Civic Data Coalition status could not be verified (GitHub API blocked; search returned only links). UNVERIFIED recollection: the `django-calaccess-raw-data` / `django-calaccess-processed-data` projects and the `calaccess.californiacivicdata.org` downloads site stopped regular daily processing around 2018-2019 and are effectively unmaintained; treat any processed files as stale and prefer the SOS zip. Verify at `github.com/california-civic-data-coalition` (last-commit dates).
- No SOS statement was found on whether CARS will offer a bulk export/API.
- I found no authoritative single sentence from SOS saying "Cal-Access excludes local filings"; the conclusion rests on the filing-officer structure and the SF/SOS pages above.

## 4. FPPC resources: Form 496 rules, local portal list, statewide aggregation of local filings (AB 2151, SB 1239)

### Takeaway
There is no statewide machine-readable aggregation of local campaign filings in California; FPPC and SOS only maintain link directories, and AB 2151 merely obliges local agencies to post filings online. Form 496 gives exactly what the project needs (dated, candidate/measure-specific, support/oppose-coded IE amounts) but only for IEs of $1,000+ within the 90 days before an election.

### Cited Findings
- Form 496 trigger: independent expenditures totalling $1,000 or more during the 90-day election cycle (90 days before the election through election day); due within 24 hours. — [FPPC Manual 1, Ch. 12](https://fppc.ca.gov/content/dam/fppc/NS-Documents/TAD/Campaign%20Manuals/Manual_1/Manual-1-Chapter-12-Reporting-Independent-Expenditures.pdf)
- Form 496 content: candidate or measure supported/opposed, office sought/held, district number, support vs oppose; date, type (description) and amount of each expenditure plus cumulative-to-date; contributions of $100+ received since the last Form 460 closing date. A separate 496 is required per candidate/measure with costs prorated. — [FPPC Manual 1, Ch. 12](https://fppc.ca.gov/content/dam/fppc/NS-Documents/TAD/Campaign%20Manuals/Manual_1/Manual-1-Chapter-12-Reporting-Independent-Expenditures.pdf)
- Additional 496 trigger: within 10 business days when IEs of $5,000+ support or oppose qualification of a single local initiative or referendum. — [FPPC Manual 1, Ch. 12](https://fppc.ca.gov/content/dam/fppc/NS-Documents/TAD/Campaign%20Manuals/Manual_1/Manual-1-Chapter-12-Reporting-Independent-Expenditures.pdf)
- Form 462 (Verification of Independent Expenditures): required once IEs of $1,000+ in a calendar year for a single candidate/measure; emailed to FPPC within 10 days; certifies non-coordination. — [FPPC Manual 1, Ch. 12](https://fppc.ca.gov/content/dam/fppc/NS-Documents/TAD/Campaign%20Manuals/Manual_1/Manual-1-Chapter-12-Reporting-Independent-Expenditures.pdf)
- For local contests the 496 goes to the local filing officer: SF's dashboards include committees that "filed a Form 496 with the SFEC". — [About the SF dashboards](https://sfethics.org/disclosures/campaign-finance-disclosure/campaign-finance-dashboards/dashboards-about-the-dashboards)
- AB 2151 (Gallagher, Ch. 214, Stats. 2020): local agencies must post campaign statements and reports filed on paper on their website within 72 hours of the filing deadline; FPPC's AB 2151 page is a list of links to local sites, not a database. — [FPPC: AB 2151 Local Campaign Filings](https://www.fppc.ca.gov/learn/campaign-rules/ab2151.html)
- SOS likewise offers only a link directory of city/county e-filing sites. — [SOS: City and County Electronic Filings](https://www.sos.ca.gov/campaign-lobbying/cal-access-resources/city-and-county-electronic-filings)

### Inferences
- IEs under $1,000 or made more than 90 days out appear only on Form 460 Schedule D (recipient-coded, dated) or Form 465 / Form 461 for non-committee spenders; timing analyses based on 496 alone are left-truncated at E-90.
- AB 2151 postings are mostly scanned PDFs, so "public" does not mean "usable in two days" outside NetFile/Socrata jurisdictions.
- Member communications and issue ads are not IEs and will be missing from any of this.

- FPPC "top contributor lists" cover state ballot measures/candidates (UNVERIFIED recollection); no local equivalent found. `fppc.ca.gov/transparency/campaign-disclosure-portals.html` could not be fetched.
- Form 465 and Form 461 rules were not covered by the fetched chapter.

## 5. Third-party aggregations (OpenSecrets/FollowTheMoney, DIME, MapLight, CalMatters, local news trackers) and outcome data

### Takeaway
No third-party aggregator was confirmed to offer downloadable Bay Area local IE data; the most useful third-party asset found is on the outcome side: the American Local Government Elections Database (57,139 contests, 1989-2021). Aggregators mostly stop at state level.

### Cited Findings

## 6. Prior empirical research on IE/spending effects and identification strategies

### Takeaway
Credible designs in the literature rely on policy shocks (Citizens United) across many states and thousands of races, or matched-city comparisons; purely local studies have tiny samples (n about 22 cities) and mostly document that IEs follow competitive races. A 2-day Bay Area study will be descriptive/correlational at best unless it exploits precinct-level and timing variation.

### Cited Findings
- Klumpp, Mialon and Williams (2016), "The Business of American Democracy: Citizens United, Independent Spending, and Elections", Journal of Law and Economics 59(1): Citizens United is associated with about a 4 percentage-point increase in Republican election probabilities in state house races, 10+ points in several states; also examines reelection rates, candidate entry and direct contributions. — [Chicago Unbound abstract](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/jle/vol59/iss1/1/)
- Common Cause (2023): matched difference-in-differences style comparison of 11 treated vs 11 control California cities around AB 571; no evidence the default contribution limit raised IEs; authors caution n=22 and most differences not statistically significant; they attribute control-city IE growth to possibly more competitive elections. — [Common Cause AB 571 report](https://www.commoncause.org/california/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2024/02/CA_AB571_MiniReportv2.pdf)
- Heerwig/Campbell via Sightline (2025): interest groups spend more IEs when elections are more competitive; a preliminary regression controlling for competition finds Seattle's voucher programme "does not seem to increase the level of independent expenditures"; IEs rose in Oakland, San Diego and Spokane mayoral races similarly or more sharply. Sightline helped design the voucher programme (interested party) and the regression is described as preliminary. — [Sightline, June 2025](https://www.sightline.org/2025/06/17/in-seattle-elections-dark-money-follows-competition-not-democracy-vouchers/)
- The ALGED authors provide the outcome infrastructure used by recent local-elections RD papers (57,139 contests). — [Scientific Data article](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41597-023-02792-x)

### Inferences
- The central endogeneity problem is documented even in the advocacy-side sources: IEs flow to competitive races, so naive regressions of vote share on IE dollars confound closeness with money. Any Bay Area estimate needs at minimum contest fixed effects plus a pre-IE baseline for candidate strength.
- Feasible designs with the data found: (a) precinct panel within contest: precinct vote share for candidate vs IE mail/field targeting is NOT observable from 496s (no geography), so precinct variation only helps via heterogeneous exposure proxies; (b) timing: daily Form 496 series vs vote-by-mail return timing (early vs late ballots) within the same contest; (c) ranked-choice contests in SF/Oakland/Berkeley give round-by-round transfers; (d) cross-contest panel with candidate's own contributions and incumbency as controls. All are suggestive, none cleanly causal. These design ideas are mine, not sourced.
- Sample-size reality check from sources: state-level studies pool all state house races across states and years; local studies have 22 cities; SF offers about 10-13 candidate contests per November. Expect wide confidence intervals.
