## 1. Appointment rules, officers, quorum and voting thresholds

### Takeaway
Since Proposition D (March 2002), the Commission has seven members: four nominated by the Mayor and three by the President of the Board of Supervisors, all subject to Board approval, serving four-year terms. Official acts need four affirmative votes regardless of attendance, so a 3-3 or 3-2 vote fails, and a failed motion is treated as disapproval of the requested action (a CU is denied; a DR is "not taken" and the project stands as proposed) unless a substitute motion such as a continuance passes.

### Cited Findings
- Charter 4.105: seven members; Mayor nominates four, President of the Board of Supervisors nominates three; emphasis on geographic diversity; vacancies filled by the appointing officer. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206)
- Every nomination (Mayor's or Board President's) needs Board of Supervisors approval after a public hearing and vote within 60 days of transmittal to the Clerk; if the Board does not act in 60 days the nominee is deemed approved. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206); [BOS Planning Commission summary sheet](https://www.sfbos.org/sites/default/files/Planning_Commission.pdf)
- Transition: existing members' terms ended at noon on July 1, 2002. Initial terms were staggered by lot (three members got two-year terms: two mayoral, one Board President appointee; four got four-year terms). All later terms are four years. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206)
- Removal: only by the appointing officer, under Charter 15.105. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206)
- Authority is listed as "Charter Section 4.105 (Prop D; March 5, 2002 Election)"; holdover after term expiry is limited to 60 days (Charter 4.101.5). Rules Committee hears nominees and forwards to the full Board. — [BOS Planning Commission summary sheet](https://www.sfbos.org/sites/default/files/Planning_Commission.pdf)
- Later changes: the section's history note reads "Amended March 2002; Amended by Proposition J, 11/4/2008". No change to the 4/3 split was found. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206)
- Term cycles: seats expire around June 30 / July 1 in even years on two alternating cycles (2026 and 2028 in the Dec 2024 sheet: seats 2, 3, 5, 7 expiring 2026; seats 1, 4, 6 expiring 2028). Seats 1-3 are Board President seats; seats 4-7 are mayoral. — [BOS Planning Commission summary sheet](https://www.sfbos.org/sites/default/files/Planning_Commission.pdf)
- Director of Planning: the Commission submits at least three qualified candidates to the Mayor. The Zoning Administrator is appointed by the Director and decides variances; ZA appeals go to the Board of Appeals within 10 days. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206)
- Conditional use decisions by the Commission can be overturned by the Board of Supervisors on appeal by a two-thirds vote. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206)
- Officers: President and Vice President are elected by the Commission itself at the first regular meeting on or after January 15 each year (or a later meeting), for one-year terms; first nominee to get a majority wins. Both officers cannot be mayoral appointees. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Quorum: a majority of all members (four). — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Votes: procedural matters (including continuances) need a majority of members present when a quorum is present. Official acts (Motions, Resolutions) need a majority of all members, i.e. four votes. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission); [Hearing Procedures (CPC)](https://sfplanning.org/resource/hearing-procedures-cpc)
- Failed motion: a motion with fewer than four votes fails and results in disapproval of the requested action unless a substitute motion (continuance or other action) is adopted. Rule examples: a Conditional Use without four votes to approve is denied; a Discretionary Review without four votes proceeds without modification. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- DR specifically: the Commission must vote to "Take DR" to disapprove or modify a permit; if that motion fails, the project is approved as proposed. — [Hearing Procedures (CPC)](https://sfplanning.org/resource/hearing-procedures-cpc)
- Motion of intent: when a majority of all members passes a motion contrary to staff's preliminary recommendation and no findings have been prepared to support it. The final motion with findings comes back later; absent commissioners must review the materials and recording before voting on the final motion; the applicant cannot withdraw between the intent vote and the final written motion. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Rules were last amended December 16, 2021 (page updated June 2023). — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Appeal routes: CU/PUD to Board of Supervisors within 30 days; DR decisions via the building permit to the Board of Appeals within 15 days of permit issuance/denial; variances to the Board of Appeals within 10 days of the decision letter. — [Hearing Procedures (CPC)](https://sfplanning.org/resource/hearing-procedures-cpc)

### Inferences
- A 3-3 vote (one member absent or recused) or a 3-2 vote is not a "tie" in effect: the motion on the floor simply fails. What that means for the project depends on which way the motion was framed. A failed motion to approve a CU = denial; a failed motion to take DR = project approved as proposed. Analysts should therefore code the motion text, not just the tally. Votes such as 4-2, 4-1 and 4-0 pass; 3-x never passes on an official act.
- Because continuances are procedural, they can pass with three votes when only four or five members are present. A continuance is the obvious escape valve when neither side has four votes, so short-handed meetings should show more continuances.
- "Motion of intent to disapprove" in the dataset should mean: staff recommended approval, at least four commissioners voted to go the other way, and the item was continued to a later date for adoption of written findings. Expect a matching follow-up item a few weeks later with the final disapproval motion. The mirror case ("intent to approve" against a staff denial recommendation) also exists.
- The rule that both officers cannot be mayoral appointees means one officer always comes from the Board President's three seats (consistent with Campbell/Koppel in 2026, So/Moore in 2025, Tanner/Moore in 2022).
- Two term cycles expiring at end of June in even years means membership turnover clusters in July-September of even years (2020, 2024 and 2026 show this), and summer meetings in those years can be short-handed.

### Gaps

- What Proposition J (Nov 2008) changed in 4.105 was not confirmed from a fetched source (UNVERIFIED background knowledge: it created the Historic Preservation Commission and made conforming edits).
- Whether any 2024-2026 charter measure (e.g. commission-reform measures on the Nov 2024 ballot, or the 2026 "Strengthening Executive Branch Accountability" charter amendment draft that appeared in search results) changed Planning Commission appointment or removal rules was not checked. The current amlegal text still shows the 4/3 structure.
- The Rules' exact wording on tie votes was not found; the four-vote rule covers it by implication.

## 3. Hearing format, calendar sections and continuances

### Takeaway
Hearings are weekly on Thursdays in City Hall Room 400, with a fixed lettered calendar: A. Items Proposed for Continuance, then (when used) Consent Calendar, Commission Matters, Department Matters, General Public Comment, Regular Calendar, and Discretionary Review Calendar. The start time has shifted over the period (noon in 2014, 1:00 p.m. in 2018-2022, noon again by 2025-2026), which matters for any time-of-day analysis.

### Cited Findings
- Rules-prescribed order of business: Roll Call; Consent Calendar; Commission Matters; Action Item List; Department Matters; Public Comment (15 min); Regular Calendar; Discretionary Review Calendar; Public Comment; Adjournment. The President/Chair may change the order. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Actual agenda lettering, 2014 minutes: A. Consideration of Items Proposed for Continuance; B. Commission Matters (minutes, commissioner comments); C. Department Matters (Director's announcements; review of past events at BOS, Board of Appeals, HPC); D. General Public Comment (15 min); E. Regular Calendar; F. Discretionary Review Calendar; G. Public Comment; Adjournment. — [CPC minutes 3/13/2014](https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/default/files/meetingarchive/planning_dept/commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20140313_cal.min.pdf)
- 2020-2025 agendas insert "B. Consent Calendar" after continuances when there are consent items, shifting later letters (C. Commission Matters, D. Department Matters, E. General Public Comment, F. Regular Calendar, G. Discretionary Review Calendar). Agendas without consent items (9/6/2018, 10/15/2026) omit the section and run A-F. — [CPC agenda 2/10/2022](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2022-02/20220210_cal%20(ID%201276007).pdf); [CPC agenda 9/6/2018](https://commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20180906_cal.pdf); [CPC agenda 10/15/2026](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2026-10/20261015_cpc%20%28ID%201591031%29.pdf)
- Consent Calendar: items are approved by a single vote with no separate discussion; a Commissioner, a member of the public, or the Director can pull an item to the Regular Calendar. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Continuance calendar boilerplate: "The Commission may continue an item to the proposed date, continue it to another date, or hear it on this calendar." Items are listed as "Proposed for Continuance to [date]"; items already withdrawn are listed there marked "WITHDRAWN". — [CPC agenda 9/6/2018](https://commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20180906_cal.pdf); [CPC agenda 10/15/2026](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2026-10/20261015_cpc%20%28ID%201591031%29.pdf)
- Agendas do not state who asked for the continuance or why. Example 4/10/2025: six items proposed for continuance (five to the following week), three of which had already been continued from earlier hearings. — [CPC agenda 4/10/2025](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2025-04/20250410_cpc%20(ID%201504528).pdf)
- Members of the public do speak on continuance items and ask for longer continuances: on 3/13/2014 four neighbourhood speakers asked for two-week to six-week continuances of a zoning text amendment citing lack of outreach; the item was "Continued to May 1, 2014" by a 7-0 vote. — [CPC minutes 3/13/2014](https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/default/files/meetingarchive/planning_dept/commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20140313_cal.min.pdf)
- Continuances are procedural: a majority of those present suffices (with quorum). A continuance can also be adopted as a substitute motion after a main motion fails. A hearing motion may "approve, approve with conditions or amendments, disapprove, or continue to another date". — [Hearing Procedures (CPC)](https://sfplanning.org/resource/hearing-procedures-cpc); [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Meeting day/place: Thursdays, Room 400 City Hall; a fifth Thursday in a month is normally cancelled; meetings start no earlier than noon. Agendas are posted six calendar days before each hearing. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission); [sfplanning.org Planning Commission page](https://sfplanning.org/planning-commission)
- Start times observed: 12:00 noon (called to order 12:08 p.m.) on 3/13/2014; 1:00 p.m. on 9/6/2018, 5/28/2020, 2/10/2022; 12:00 p.m. on 4/10/2025 and 10/15/2026. — [2014 minutes](https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/default/files/meetingarchive/planning_dept/commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20140313_cal.min.pdf); [2018 agenda](https://commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20180906_cal.pdf); [2020 agenda](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2020-05/20200528_cal.pdf); [2022 agenda](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2022-02/20220210_cal%20(ID%201276007).pdf); [2025 agenda](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2025-04/20250410_cpc%20(ID%201504528).pdf); [2026 agenda](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2026-10/20261015_cpc%20%28ID%201591031%29.pdf)
- Remote era: hearings went remote (video/teleconference with call-in comment) from April 3, 2020; the 5/28/2020 agenda had a time-certain section "G. 3:00 P.M." for later items instead of a labelled DR calendar. — [CPC agenda 5/28/2020](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2020-05/20200528_cal.pdf)
- Regular Calendar format (CU, PUD, Sec. 309 etc.): staff presentation; sponsor up to 10 min (15 with advance request); organised opposition up to 10 min (advance request, at least three speakers); public 3 min each; then deliberation and motion. — [Hearing Procedures (CPC)](https://sfplanning.org/resource/hearing-procedures-cpc)
- DR Calendar format: staff; DR requestor 5 min each; supporters of DR 3 min each; sponsor 5 min (10 if multiple requestors); supporters of project 3 min each; 2-min rebuttals. Mandatory DR (e.g. dwelling unit removal): sponsor 5 min, public 3 min each. — [Commission Rules & Regulations](https://sfplanning.org/resource/rules-regulations-san-francisco-planning-commission)
- Minutes record each action as text plus AYES / NAYES / ABSENT / RECUSED name lists and a Motion, Resolution or DRA number; e.g. "Took DR and required the project to square off the 4th FL" (AYES six, RECUSED Fong; DRA No. 0355); "Adopted a Recommendation for Approval with Modifications" (6-1, Antonini against; Resolution 19101). Items can be "called out of order". — [CPC minutes 3/13/2014](https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/default/files/meetingarchive/planning_dept/commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20140313_cal.min.pdf)
- Agenda items carry staff's "Preliminary Recommendation" (e.g. "Approve", "Deny", "Do Not Take DR and Approve"). — [CPC agenda 10/15/2026](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2026-10/20261015_cpc%20%28ID%201591031%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- Position on the agenda is confounded with item type. DR items are always last, the Regular Calendar comes before them, and continuance-calendar items are first. "Late items are continued more often" may partly reflect that DRs (neighbour disputes prone to negotiated delay) sit at the end, plus fatigue or loss of quorum late in long hearings. Compare within section, and use item sequence within the Regular Calendar.
- There are two distinct kinds of continuance in the data: (1) items on the "Items Proposed for Continuance" calendar, agreed before the hearing and disposed of in minutes at the top of the meeting, usually without a hearing on the merits; (2) items continued from the floor of the Regular or DR calendar after being heard. They should be coded separately; only type 2 can be related to lateness or deliberation.
- "Without hearing" in minutes most plausibly marks type-1 dispositions or items continued/withdrawn before any staff presentation or testimony (inference from the agenda structure; no official definition found).
- Since agendas give no requester, attribution of continuance requests has to come from staff memos or hearing video, not the agenda. The appearance of a continuance-calendar item with a staff recommendation of "Deny" (85 Liberty St, 10/15/2026) is consistent with sponsors seeking time to revise a project facing an adverse recommendation.
- Start-time changes (noon vs 1 p.m.) mean elapsed-time measures should be computed from the actual call-to-order time in each set of minutes, not assumed.
- Section letters are not stable identifiers (the Regular Calendar is "E" or "F" depending on whether a Consent Calendar exists); parse by section title.

### Gaps
- No official statement was found of who typically requests placement on the continuance calendar. UNVERIFIED background understanding: most are requested by the project sponsor (often to keep negotiating with neighbours or DR requestors, or to revise plans) or by staff (noticing errors, incomplete analysis, pending legislation or supervisor amendments); DR requestors and neighbours also ask, and commissioners can continue items from the dais, including for lack of a full commission. This should be checked against a sample of hearing videos or staff continuance memos.
- "Continued indefinitely": no official definition was fetched. UNVERIFIED background understanding: the item is taken off calendar with no return date and must be re-noticed before it can be heard again; many such items never return (project withdrawn or redesigned). The 2018 and 2025 agendas sampled contained no "Proposed for Indefinite Continuance" item to quote.
- "Without hearing": no official definition found (see inference above).
- The Rules' text on any hard stop time for hearings was not found; the Rules set only an earliest start. Whether the Commission has an informal practice of not calling new items after a certain hour is unknown.
- The "Action Item List" in the Rules' order of business does not appear as a lettered section in the agendas sampled; its practical use is unclear.
- Exact dates when the start time moved from noon to 1 p.m. and back were not established (somewhere between 2014 and 2018, and between 2022 and 2025).

## 4. Zoning Administrator on joint variance items

### Takeaway
Variances are decided by the Zoning Administrator, not the Commission, so when a project needs both a Commission approval (CU or DR) and a variance, the two are calendared as paired "a/b" items and heard together; the Commission votes on its part and the ZA separately closes the variance hearing and usually takes it under advisement, with the binding decision issued later by letter. Those "b" items therefore have no commissioner vote and should be excluded from vote analysis.

### Cited Findings
- The Charter assigns variance decisions to the Zoning Administrator (appointed by the Planning Director), with required findings on exceptional circumstances, hardship, property rights, public welfare and consistency with the zoning ordinance; appeals go to the Board of Appeals within ten days. — [SF Charter, amlegal](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/latest/sf_charter/0-0-0-206)
- At a variance hearing the ZA "may make a decision, take the matter under advisement, or continue it for more information"; "No decision is final until a Variance Decision Letter is issued"; letters can take up to two months after the hearing. — [Hearing Procedures - Zoning Variance](https://sfplanning.org/resource/hearing-procedures-zoning-variance)
- Example of the paired pattern: 3/13/2014 item 11a (Commission) "Approved with Conditions as amended", 7-0, Motion 19103; item 11b (variance) "Acting ZA closed the PH and took the matter under advisement", with no vote recorded. The ZA (Scott Sanchez) is listed among staff present. — [CPC minutes 3/13/2014](https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/default/files/meetingarchive/planning_dept/commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20140313_cal.min.pdf)
- Agendas list "Variance (Zoning Administrator action)", case suffix VAR, 10-calendar-day appeal to the Board of Appeals running from issuance of the decision letter. — [CPC agenda 4/10/2025](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2025-04/20250410_cpc%20(ID%201504528).pdf)
- The ZA also holds separate standalone monthly variance hearings with their own regular, consent and continuance calendars. — [Hearing Procedures - Zoning Variance](https://sfplanning.org/resource/hearing-procedures-zoning-variance)

### Inferences
- "ZA closed the public hearing and took the matter under advisement" means: testimony on the variance is finished, the ZA has not announced a decision, and the outcome will appear only in a later Variance Decision Letter outside the Commission's minutes. Taking under advisement lets the ZA align the variance with whatever conditions the Commission imposed on the companion item.
- Related phrasings to expect: "ZA indicated an intent to grant/deny", "ZA continued the matter to [date]" (when the Commission continues the companion item), or "Acting ZA" when the Assistant ZA sits in.
- In the dataset, VAR-suffix items will have empty aye/nay fields by design; they are not unanimous votes or missing data. The companion "a" item carries the vote.

### Gaps
- No official page describing the joint-hearing practice in general terms was found; the description rests on the Charter division of authority plus the minutes example.
- Outcomes of variances taken under advisement are not in Commission minutes; they would have to be pulled from decision letters (Planning's Property Information Map / Accela records).

## 5. Published analyses of voting patterns, hearing length and late-night hearings

### Takeaway
I found no systematic published quantitative analysis of Planning Commission roll-call votes or hearing length; what exists is journalism on individual marathon hearings and on the Commission's shrinking docket, plus one advocacy piece on commissioner demographics. A vote-level study by the student team would likely be novel.

### Cited Findings
- Mission Local (July 2026, "Things are getting boring at the Planning Commission"): seven weekly meetings cancelled in three months; state streamlining laws (SB 423, AB 2011) and the 2026 western-neighbourhoods rezoning move many housing projects to ministerial approval without a Commission hearing. Dan Sider (Planning chief of staff) said state laws "really changed the Planning Commission" toward major projects and policy; SPUR's Annie Fryman said it "has become a less consequential body in shaping development". The article gives no hearing-length or vote statistics. — [Mission Local 7/9/2026](https://missionlocal.org/2026/07/san-francisco-planning-commission-streamlining/)
- Under SB 423, some projects now appear on the Commission agenda only as informational presentations with no vote (e.g. 2213 25th Street, 10/15/2026, "SB 423 informational presentation", no recommendation). — [CPC agenda 10/15/2026](https://sfplanning.org/sites/default/files/agendas/2026-10/20261015_cpc%20%28ID%201591031%29.pdf)
- Marathon hearing example: the September 11, 2025 upzoning hearing ran more than 10 hours, with over five hours of public comment, ending at 10:10 p.m.; vote 4-3. — [Mission Local 9/12/2025](https://missionlocal.org/2025/09/sf-upzoning-plan-passes-commission/)
- Ordinary long hearing example: March 13, 2014 ran 12:08 p.m. to 9:04 p.m. (about nine hours) with six regular-calendar and two DR items. — [CPC minutes 3/13/2014](https://sfplanning.s3.amazonaws.com/default/files/meetingarchive/planning_dept/commissions.sfplanning.org/cpcpackets/20140313_cal.min.pdf)
- Advocacy analysis (Steven Buss, Medium, c. 2018, pro-housing perspective): compared commissioners' demographics and home locations to the city (one renter among seven vs about 65% renters citywide; no commissioner living in the five eastern neighbourhoods holding about 60% of affordable units). It does not analyse votes or hearing length. — [Medium, "The staggering inequity of the San Francisco Planning Commission"](https://medium.com/@sbuss/the-staggering-inequity-of-the-san-francisco-planning-commission-a546b82c5d56)
- Tim Redmond's claim of consistent 4-3 appointer-line splits "on key housing votes" is a journalist's characterisation, not a count. — [48 Hills 7/27/2026](https://48hills.org/2026/07/whats-up-with-our-new-planning-commission-members/)

### Inferences
- The composition of the docket changes over the study period: post-2023 state streamlining removes many code-compliant housing projects from the hearing docket, so later-year agendas are shorter and weighted toward policy items, CUs for non-housing uses, and DRs. Trends in split-vote or continuance rates across years should control for this compositional shift and for the 2020-2022 remote-hearing period.
- Minutes record call-to-order and adjournment times, so the team can construct hearing length directly from the same source as the votes.
- The hypothesis that appointer predicts vote can be tested directly; published reporting supplies the prior (appointer-line 4-3 splits on high-salience housing policy in 2023-2025) but no baseline rate.

### Gaps
- No academic paper, Controller/BLA audit, civil grand jury report or data-journalism piece quantifying Commission vote splits, continuance rates or hearing duration was found in the searches run; I did not search SPUR, the Budget and Legislative Analyst, or academic databases specifically, so absence is not established.
- A San Francisco Chronicle analysis of Board of Supervisors voting coalitions appeared in search results; it concerns the Board, not the Commission, and was not read.
- 48 Hills (July 2023) reported a "narrow commission nod" for a Breed housing ordinance; the article was not fetched, so the tally and names are unconfirmed.
